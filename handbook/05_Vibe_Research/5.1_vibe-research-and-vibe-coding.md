## Code of Conduct

1. We allow and encourage the reasonable use of AI or LLMs as assistive tools, including but not limited to:
   a. Literature search and organization;
   b. Coding assistance and debugging suggestions;
   c. Language polishing and improving expression.
2. Academic ideas, research questions, research design, technical approach, experimental plans, core conclusions, and innovations must be produced and fully understood by the student/advisor. AI / LLMs may only provide supporting assistance; leadership of the research or key academic judgments must not be handed over to AI tools.
3. For all content generated or partly generated by AI / LLMs, students must verify its accuracy item by item and ensure it is fully consistent with the actual research process, experimental results, and facts. Using AI tools must not introduce errors, exaggeration, inconsistencies, or descriptions that cannot be reproduced.
4. Do not use AI / LLMs to generate, fabricate, or guess academic literature, reference entries, or citation information. All cited literature must come from real, verifiable sources, and the student must personally complete the search, reading, and confirmation.
5. Do not use AI / LLMs for any form of academic misconduct, including but not limited to:
   a. Generating false data, experimental results, or citations;
   b. Forging, tampering with, or fabricating experimental processes;
   c. Concealing plagiarism of, or improper paraphrasing of, others' work.
6. For academic paper submissions, thesis writing, or other formal academic outputs, if a journal, conference, or university has explicit disclosure or restriction requirements for AI use, follow those rules strictly; if in doubt, communicate with your advisor in advance.

## Background

### A Revolution Sparked by a Tweet

In February 2025, Andrej Karpathy, former Tesla AI director and OpenAI co-founder, posted a tweet on social media:

> There's a new kind of coding I call "vibe coding", where you fully give in to the vibes, embrace exponentials, and forget that the code even exists.

The reason this tweet sparked such wide discussion is that we are entering an era in which you can "make" software without "writing" code.

### "Vibe Coding" Becomes the Word of the Year

In November 2025, Collins Dictionary announced "Vibe Coding" as its Word of the Year.

The official definition of the term is:

> The use of artificial intelligence prompted by natural language to write computer code.

In other words, you do not need to learn any programming language; just tell the AI in natural language, "I want an XX", and it can build it for you.

### The Data Doesn't Lie

**A Striking Finding from Y Combinator**

In March 2025, Garry Tan, CEO of Y Combinator, the world's most famous startup accelerator, revealed:

In the latest batch of startups, 25% of the companies reported that 95% of their code was generated by AI.

These are not hobby side projects but real startups that are raising money and growing. Even more surprising, **these companies' teams are often smaller than 10 people**, yet they build products that used to take dozens of people.

## Vibe Coding

> The core shift of Vibe Coding: from "writing code" to "expressing things clearly + judging right from wrong"

### When We Go from Coder to Commander

**What has changed?**

| Past | Now |
|------|------|
| Memorizing syntax rules | Describing requirements clearly |
| Writing code by hand | Judging whether the code is right |
| Debugging to find Bugs | Describing the problem and letting AI fix it |
| Reading technical documentation | Knowing what to ask and what to look up |

**What has not changed?**
- You need to know what you want: however smart the AI is, it cannot figure out for you what to build
- You need to judge whether the result is good: AI may make mistakes, and you must be able to see them
- You need a problem-solving approach: when you run into a problem, know how to troubleshoot it step by step

## Methodology (Mindset and Techniques)

> We help you master the methodology of Vibe Coding from two complementary angles, "mindset" and "techniques", so you can write higher-quality code with higher efficiency.

### Mindset (Principles to Keep in Mind)

- **Whatever AI can do, do not do by hand**
- **Ask AI about every problem**
- **Let the goal lead**: every action in the development process revolves around the "goal"
- **Context is the first principle of Vibe Coding (context is king)**: "Garbage in, Garbage out"
- **Occam's razor**: do not write irrelevant code unless necessary, to reduce interference from irrelevant context
- **Subtractive thinking (MVP development)**: use the smallest investment (Minimum) to validate the core hypothesis (Visible) and deliver the smallest value (Product)

### Techniques (Tips You Can Leverage)

**1. Plan first, execute later**

In Cursor, when implementing a minimal MVP, use Plan Mode (switch with Shift+Tab) to have the AI generate a plan first, and switch to Agent Mode only after the plan looks right.
- Make sure the AI understands the idea (Idea), the implementation (Implementation), and the goal (Goal) you want to achieve
- Catch the AI's misunderstandings early and correct them promptly

**2. Clear problem requirements**

> **Clear requirements = less rework + fewer errors**

- What do you want to implement?
- What is the functionality?
- What are the I/O inputs and outputs?
- Any special code conventions, additional requirements, or constraints? For example: do not build with LangChain, and call the LLM with the native OpenAI package instead; apply parallel optimization wherever possible; and so on.

> **Do not make the AI guess what you want; tell the AI what you want to do and how it should be done**

```
I want to implement a functional module [Module Name]. The purpose of this module is [Function Description],
its input is [Input], and its output is [Output].

Notes:
- The module's input is a JSON file, and each Object has the structure {"key": value, ...}
- The module's output is a JSON file, and I can configure the save path of the output file; the output format is {"key": value, ...}
- The module needs detailed Logging; you can use the Loguru Python Package
- Use multithreaded/multiprocess parallelism to optimize execution efficiency, and I can configure the degree of parallelism
```

**3. Cost (small fast steps) <<< Cost (big strides)**

<ol type="a">
  <li>Have the AI implement one Minimum Functional Unit at a time</li>
  <li>Immediately do Code Review / Testing once it is implemented</li>
  <li><strong>‼Human supervision is still important‼</strong>
    <ol type="i">
      <li>Avoid being the last moat protecting a pile of garbage code!</li>
      <li>You do not have to read every line of code carefully, but you need to make sure the whole logic is what you want (as expected)</li>
      <li>Prevent the buildup of large amounts of unreviewed code that deviates from expectations</li>
    </ol>
  </li>
  <li>Remember: steady implementation in small steps is always better than a big stride all at once</li>
</ol>

**4. Manage context (context is king)**

<ol type="a">
  <li>Within a single Chat Session, the model easily hits the Context Limit (reaching the maximum context)
    <ol type="i">
      <li>Tools such as Cursor or Claude Code perform Context Summarization</li>
      <li><strong>However, this does not mean the information is retained losslessly!</strong></li>
      <li>Therefore, complex tasks can be split across multiple Chat Sessions</li>
    </ol>
  </li>
  <li>When you find that the AI has "forgotten" the "conventions" you asked for earlier, or is hallucinating:
    <ol type="i">
      <li>Immediately add context related to the current requirement (see point 2, "Clear problem requirements")</li>
      <li>We recommend opening a new Chat Session to keep the model's Attention on the current requirement</li>
    </ol>
  </li>
</ol>

**5. Provide sufficiently detailed error information when correcting errors**

When the AI's code fails to run, provide enough error context:

- Which code file has the error?
- What is the run command?
- What error occurred? (the Error Stack Traceback in the Terminal)

Demonstration:

```text
Wrong way to ask:
My code throws an error, help me fix it.

Right way to ask:
My Python script crashes when computing the total price. Please fix it based on the following context and error stack.

1. The code file with the error (shopping_cart.py)
2. Run command: `python shopping_cart.py`
3. Terminal error message (Error Stack Traceback):
Traceback (most recent call last):
File "shopping_cart.py", line 8, in <module>
total += item['price'] * item['qty']
TypeError: can't multiply sequence by non-int of type 'float'
```

**6. (Advanced) Version control**

a. Practice and become proficient with the Git Add/Commit/Push commands
```bash
git add <files>
git commit -m "Detailed comments"
```

b. Each time a minimum functional unit is completed, have the AI make a Git Commit with detailed Comments, for example:
```
refactor: clean up unused code and optimize app structure
- Remove unused imports and redundant functions across `app/`.
- Extract hardcoded BIRD dataset mappings to `app/db_utils/constants.py`.
- Clean up `__init__.py` exports and fix typos in `app/db_utils/schema.py`.
- Update default SQL execution timeout in `app/config/config.py`.

Co-authored-by: Cursor <cursoragent@cursor.com>
```

c. Good use of version control benefits the whole project development cycle
- The AI broke code that was originally correct → roll back the version
- Multi-branch development → team collaboration with fewer conflicts

## Vibe Figure

> For a paper (Research Project), the end of Vibe Coding marks the completion of the "Experiments".

Next we move on to writing the Paper, and the first task is the Figures, such as the Teaser Figure, Overview Figure, and Motivation Figure.

### Step 1: Figure Design

The core logic of this step is to explain the "paper logic" clearly, and then discuss the drawing details / your expectations with the LLM.

- Based on my logic, design the main figure of my paper: first propose several possible ways to draw it, and we will discuss and decide which form to use
- Based on that form, generate a Prompt that can be used directly for Gemini image generation, requiring that all text in the figure be in English, the font be [specified], the quality meet the standard of [the target conference], 8K

Readers who are skilled at hand drawing can also use some drawing tools to draw the vector figure (PDF / SVG) by hand, using the sketch as a reference.

- Figma on the web: https://www.figma.com
- OmniGraffle on MacOS
- PowerPoint (PPT)

No drawing tool is better or worse than another; the one you are proficient with is best.

#### Four-Zone Closed-Loop Figure Design

![Figure result example](./assets/5.1_vibe_research/image10.png)

**Corresponding Prompt**

```text
Please draw an academic paper Figure 2 (a two-column wide figure, landscape, 16:9 or wider), with a minimalist, publishable style (figure quality at the NeurIPS/ICLR level), flat vector, with little text and more icons; do not draw it like a "flowchart manual". Goal: use one figure to express the relationships among three kinds of scaling (ENV/TASK/TRAJ), their main deliverables, how an agent uses them to learn, and the capability gains and closed loop.

[Output requirements]
- Output: 8K quality, ultra-clear Figure
- Background: pure white
- Font: Droid Serif, with slightly bold titles
- Lines: uniform stroke width (1.2–1.5 pt recommended), uniform arrow style
- Readability: stays clear when scaled down to the width of a two-column paper (minimum font size no smaller than 8pt)
- Colors: three main colors for ENV/TASK/TRAJ (low-saturation blue, green, yellow, purple, etc.)
- Ample white space, strict grid alignment, no shadows/gradients/textures

[Overall layout: four zones + Legend]
- Left: A. Taxonomy (three swim lanes)
- Middle: B. Deliverables (three deliverables)
- Right: C. Leverage + Gains (learning-operator panel + capability ladder)
- Bottom: D. Closed-loop (a four-step closed loop spanning the whole figure)
- Corner: Legend (explains what the icons mean and what "changing/fixed" means, with minimal text)

────────────────────────────────────────
[Zone A Taxonomy (three swim lanes, minimal text)]
Draw three large horizontal bands (top to bottom), each filled with a light tint of its main color and with a dark border/title bar:
1) ENV Scaling (main color 1)
   - Write only: the title "ENV Scaling" + the corner tag "p(E)"
   - On the right, place 4 small rounded chips (each chip: a simple icon + one word):
     a) "Actions": the icon is a minimalist "palm/pointer" or "control handle" style symbol (representing actions and affordances)
     b) "Dynamics": the icon is a "circular arrow/rotation" symbol (representing transition rules)
     c) "Obs": the icon is an "eye/camera frame" symbol (representing the observation interface)
     d) "Feedback": the icon is a "bullseye/score scale" symbol (representing the reward/feedback channel)
2) TASK Scaling (main color 2)
   - Write only: the title "TASK Scaling" + the corner tag "p(x|E)"
   - 3 chips:
     a) "Goals": the icon is a "flag/finish line" symbol
     b) "Compose": the icon is a "puzzle piece/combination" symbol
     c) "Verify": the icon is a "checkmark/inspection" symbol
3) TRAJ Scaling (main color 3)
   - Write only: the title "TRAJ Scaling" + the corner tag "D={τ}"
   - 3 chips:
     a) "Rollout": the icon is a "play triangle/replay" symbol
     b) "Search": the icon is a "magnifying glass" symbol
     c) "Filter": the icon is a "funnel/filter" symbol

Note: Zone A must not contain long paragraph explanations (for example what changes/what stays fixed); put these in the Legend.

────────────────────────────────────────
[Zone B Deliverables (three boxes in the middle, strictly aligned with the three swim lanes of Zone A)]
Three rounded rectangular boxes corresponding to ENV/TASK/TRAJ, each containing only a "noun + symbol" plus a minimalist combination of icons (the icons also flat):
1) Environment Family / Generator
   - Icon: a combination of a small globe + a factory (or gear) symbol
   - Text: "{E_i} / G_env"
2) Task Suite / Generator
   - Icon: a combination of a checklist/task list + a factory (or gear) symbol
   - Text: "{x_j} / G_task"
3) Trajectory Buffer / Dataset
   - Icon: a combination of a folder/stacked cards + a data page symbol
   - Text: "D={τ}"

From each swim lane of Zone A, use a short arrow to point to the corresponding box in Zone B (arrows carry no text).

────────────────────────────────────────
[Zone C Leverage + Gains (right side)]
Split the right side into upper and lower blocks:

C1 (upper right): Learning Operators panel
- Draw four small cards (uniform style: rounded rectangle + icon + 1–2 words):
  1) Distill: the icon is a minimalist "test tube/beaker" or "distillation tower" symbol
  2) RL/Search: the icon is a "game controller/policy joystick" or "exploration compass" symbol (pick one, keep it minimalist)
  3) World Model: the icon is a "brain/grid sphere" symbol
  4) AutoLearn: the icon is a "compass/auto-tuning knob" symbol
- Connect each of the three deliverable boxes of Zone B to these cards with arrows:
  - Express the main path through "line thickness": thicker for primary connections, thinner for secondary ones
  - No arrow text, and no complicated many-to-many explanations

C2 (lower right): Capability Ladder (three-tier ladder)
- Draw a three-tier capability ladder (one short phrase per tier, as short as possible):
  1) "Robustness (in-env)"
  2) "Generalization (in-world)"
  3) "Transfer + L2L (cross-world)"
- Add a small label at the top of the ladder: "Environment Level ↑"
- Keep the ladder mostly gray, and you may use thin color bars to mark the contributions of ENV/TASK/TRAJ (very restrained, not flashy)

────────────────────────────────────────
[Zone D Bottom closed loop (spanning the whole figure, visually forming a strong closed loop)]
Draw a four-step closed loop: Generate → Interact → Learn → Expand → flow back to Generate
- One small rounded block per step (uniform style), with a single verb only:
  - "Generate": the icon is a "magic wand/generation spark" or "factory" symbol (consistent with the above)
  - "Interact": the icon is a "two-way arrow/handshake" symbol
  - "Learn": the icon is a "rising curve/graduation cap" symbol (pick one)
  - "Expand": the icon is an "outward-expanding arrow/zoom in" symbol
- Connect them with thick arrows and close the loop back to Generate (the return arrow must be obvious)
- Above the closed loop, put a short slogan (no more than 6–8 words):
  "Executable worlds scale experience."

────────────────────────────────────────
[Legend (corner legend, minimal text)]
Place it in the upper right or upper left corner (without disturbing the main body), with 3–5 short lines of explanation:
- The three main colors represent ENV/TASK/TRAJ scaling respectively
- Chips represent "dimensions of change" (actions/dynamics/obs/feedback, etc.)
- The Zone B boxes represent "primary deliverables"
- Line thickness represents "primary vs secondary usage paths"

[Overall style constraints (must be followed)]
- Each module in the main figure has at most 5–8 words; no paragraphs
- All icons use a minimalist geometric symbol style (line/flat), nothing realistic
- Alignment: the three columns A/B/C strictly aligned; A and B in one-to-one correspondence; the connections between C and B concise
- The look should be like a top-venue paper figure: restrained, clean, with a structure readable at a glance

Output the final figure directly, 8K, ultra-clear, with no explanatory text.
```

## Vibe Writing

### Writing Guidelines

- **Do not copy AI / LLM-generated Paper content directly**; students must verify its accuracy item by item and ensure it is fully consistent with the actual research process, experimental results, and facts
- **Do not let AI / LLMs write the paper's references, guess references, or cite references freely**
- AI / LLMs may only provide supporting assistance; leadership of the research and key academic judgments must not be handed over to AI tools

### Techniques

- **Context is king: provide comprehensive, detailed context**
  - The research problem, research background, existing work, existing limitations, core challenges, and the proposed method
  - Experimental data tables (in LaTeX format)
  - Paper figures (uploaded using multimodal models such as Gemini/GPT)
- **LLMs are limited to assisting with the writing approach; students must understand, verify, and write the Paper content themselves**
