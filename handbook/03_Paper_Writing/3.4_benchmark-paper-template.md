# Paper Writing: Thinking Template for Benchmark/Evaluation Papers (for Discussion)

> **Design intent**: This template is for structuring Benchmark/Evaluation papers during topic discussions, progress reports, and manuscript writing. Readers can fill in the table item by item to instantiate their ideas, which supports self-checking, alignment, and iteration. The template builds on the DIAL lab's thinking model for technical Full Papers, and is specially tuned to the structural traits of Benchmark papers recently accepted at top venues such as NeurIPS/ICLR (StatQA [NeurIPS 2024], nvBench 2.0 [NeurIPS 2025], VisJudge-Bench [ICLR 2026]).

---

## 1. Quick Positioning: Core Differences Between Benchmark/Evaluation Papers and Technique Papers

Before you start writing, you first need to be clear about the fundamental difference in narrative axis between a Benchmark paper and a Technique Paper.

| Dimension | Technique Paper | Benchmark/Evaluation Paper |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Core contribution** | New method/algorithm | New evaluation dimension / evaluation system / dataset |
| **Introduction main axis** | Key Idea / Mechanism | **Evaluation Gap + Our Benchmark Design** |
| **Role of the "problem definition"** | The Goal can be stated in one sentence | The problem definition itself is a contribution (it defines "what to evaluate" and "how to evaluate") |
| **Method section** | The core of the paper (takes the most space) | The dataset construction pipeline is the core; a companion method is a bonus |
| **Purpose of experiments** | Prove "my method is better than others" | Reveal "where the capability boundaries of existing models lie" and provide deep insights |
| **Most important figures/tables** | Method framework figure | Benchmark comparison table + Construction Pipeline figure + multi-dimensional analysis figures |

The core of a Benchmark paper is not merely "proposing a dataset" but **defining a new evaluation dimension or problem, and providing a systematic set of measurement standards and infrastructure**.

> What exactly the Gap is matters a great deal: why is this Benchmark or this Evaluation paper needed? It is very important to explain the Gap and the Motivation clearly.

## 2. The Five Core Elements of a Benchmark Paper

An excellent Benchmark paper usually contains the following five core elements. During the conception stage, make sure each element has a clear answer.

**Element 1: A clear Research Gap (evaluation blind spot).** Existing models or evaluation systems have a blind spot in some key capability. For example, StatQA points out that existing mathematical-reasoning Benchmarks focus only on computed results and neglect assessing the applicability of statistical methods; nvBench 2.0 points out that existing Text2VIS Benchmarks all follow the single-correct-answer paradigm and ignore query ambiguity; VisJudge-Bench points out that existing evaluations focus only on surface aesthetics or information comprehension and neglect a combined evaluation of Fidelity-Expressiveness-Aesthetics.

**Element 2: A systematic construction method (Construction Pipeline).** How to build the dataset in a way that is high-quality, scalable, and low-cost. All three papers adopt innovative automated or semi-automated construction pipelines. StatQA uses "reverse synthesis" (set the answer first, then generate the question); nvBench 2.0 uses "controllable ambiguity injection" (start from unambiguous seeds and inject ambiguity); VisJudge-Bench uses "adaptive question generation + three-stage expert annotation".

**Element 3: A multi-dimensional evaluation framework (Evaluation Framework).** Beyond overall metrics, there should be a fine-grained taxonomy (Taxonomy) that can diagnose the models' capability boundaries along different sub-dimensions. For example, StatQA divides statistical tasks into 5 categories and sets Easy/Hard difficulty levels; nvBench 2.0 defines 4 ambiguity types and 5 ambiguity levels; VisJudge-Bench builds 6 measurable sub-dimensions under the three dimensions of Fidelity-Expressiveness-Aesthetics.

**Element 4: Deep empirical insights (Empirical Findings).** Experiments are more than producing scores (a Leaderboard); more importantly, through multi-angle comparisons (Human vs. LLM, different model architectures, distributions of different error types), they reveal the models' inherent weaknesses and point out directions for future research. All three papers distill explicit Findings from their experiments and discuss Research Opportunities.

**Element 5: A companion validation method (Optional but Highly Recommended).** Beyond evaluation, if you can propose a dedicated optimized model based on the Benchmark and show that it significantly improves that capability, it will greatly strengthen the depth and value of the paper. nvBench 2.0 proposes Step-Text2Vis (based on Step-DPO preference optimization); VisJudge-Bench proposes VisJudge (based on GRPO reinforcement-learning fine-tuning).

## 3. Flowchart for Writing/Conceiving the Introduction (Benchmark Edition)

The Introduction of a Benchmark paper can be regarded as the "compressed version" of the whole paper. Unlike a Technique Paper, the main axis of a Benchmark paper's Introduction is not "Key Idea / Mechanism" but **"Evaluation Gap + Benchmark Design Rationale"**. We recommend unfolding it along the following logical chain.

### 3.1 Flowchart Overview

```
Research Background → Running Example → Limitations of Existing Benchmarks → Research Gap/Questions
    → Design Considerations → Our Proposal → Contributions
```

### 3.2 Detailed Description of Each Stage

| Logical Stage | Core Content and Goal | Writing Tips | Reference Cases |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Research background and core scenario** | Introduce the research subject and explain why the task matters in the real world. | Use a carefully designed **Running Example (Figure 1)** to show intuitively the complexity of the task and how human experts handle it. Figure 1 is one of the most important figures in the paper and must be polished with great care. | StatQA: the full workflow diagram of a statistical analysis task; nvBench 2.0: multiple valid interpretations of an ambiguous query; VisJudge-Bench: positive/negative example comparisons for Fidelity/Expressiveness/Aesthetics. |
| **2. Limitations of existing Benchmarks (The Gap)** | Summarize the blind spots of existing Benchmarks or evaluation systems, usually no more than 3 points. | Stress "which key dimension existing work ignores". **We strongly recommend providing a Benchmark comparison table in the Introduction or Related Work**, comparing feature dimensions one by one so the Gap is obvious at a glance. | nvBench 2.0: Table 1 compares 7 Benchmarks on 6 dimensions; VisJudge-Bench: Table 1 compares 7 Benchmarks on 3 evaluation dimensions. |
| **3. Core research questions (Research Questions)** | Turn the Gap into 2-3 concrete research questions. | The RQs should cover: (1) How do we build a suitable Benchmark? (2) Where are the capability boundaries of existing models? (3) What are the gaps/differences between models and human experts? | StatQA: Q1 How to evaluate? Q2 How capable are LLMs? Q3 Human vs. LLM? |
| **4. Design Considerations** | State clearly the design requirements the Benchmark must meet. | This is a paragraph specific to Benchmark papers. Before formally presenting the solution, first explain "what properties a good Benchmark should have", which provides a justification for the subsequent design. | nvBench 2.0: "This benchmark should include diverse ambiguous queries, multiple valid outputs, reasoning paths, and broad domain coverage." |
| **5. Our Proposal** | Formally introduce the Benchmark proposed in this paper and its core features. | Stress the Benchmark's scale, diversity, and above all the central construction innovation. Summarize the core idea of the Pipeline in one or two sentences. | StatQA: "Our key idea is to reverse this process by synthesizing the question Q based on target answers A." |
| **6. Closing with contributions (Contributions)** | Summarize the 3-4 core contributions of the paper. | Typical structure: (1) the Benchmark itself (scale, properties); (2) the innovation in the construction Pipeline; (3) systematic evaluation and deep Findings; (4) a companion optimized model (if any). Mark the corresponding section number after each contribution. | All three papers use this structure. |

### 3.3 Key Figures and Tables in the Introduction

| Element | Purpose | Suggested Position |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Figure 1: Running Example** | Use a concrete example to show intuitively the complexity of the task and the necessity of the evaluation. | The top of page 1 or page 2. |
| **Table 1: Benchmark comparison table** | Compare existing Benchmarks along feature dimensions one by one, highlighting this paper's differentiated advantages. | The end of the Introduction, or in Related Work. |

## 4. Planning the Main Section Structure

Drawing on the common patterns of the three top-venue papers, we recommend the following section structure for the whole paper.

### Section 2: The Proposed Benchmark (the dataset proper, about 3-4 pages)

This is the core section of a Benchmark paper, equivalent to the method section of a Technique Paper.

**2.1 Design Goals & Task Scope.** State the design goals of the Benchmark and the boundaries of the task. Design goals usually include: (G1) high coverage/representativeness; (G2) support for fine-grained evaluation; (G3) low labor cost/scalability; (G4) high data quality. The task boundary needs to define clearly "what is evaluated" and "what is not evaluated".

**2.2 Benchmark Construction Pipeline.** This is the most central technical section. Describe in detail how the data is built step by step. **It must come with a clear Pipeline diagram (usually Figure 2)**. The Pipeline usually contains the following stages:

| Stage | Description | Writing Points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Data collection/preprocessing | Where does the raw data come from? How is it filtered and cleaned? | Explain the diversity and authenticity of the data sources. |
| Core synthesis/transformation logic | The innovative core of the Pipeline. How are Benchmark samples generated from the raw data? | This is the part reviewers care about most. Explain the input, output, and key operations of each step in detail. |
| Quality control and validation | How is data quality ensured? | Usually automated checks + human expert review. Report inter-annotator agreement (e.g., Cohen's Kappa, ICC). |

**2.3 Benchmark Characteristics.** Use rich figures and tables to show the statistical characteristics of the dataset.

| Content to Present | Recommended Chart Types |
| --- | --- |
| Task/type distribution | Pie chart, donut chart, bar chart |
| Difficulty/ambiguity-level distribution | Bar chart |
| Data scale statistics | Table (rows, columns, number of samples, etc.) |
| Comparison with existing Benchmarks | Feature comparison table (Table 1) |
| Data examples | A complete annotated example figure |

### Section 3: Specialized Model / Methodology (optional, about 1-2 pages)

If the paper proposes not only a Benchmark but also an optimization method for the problem, introduce it here. Explain how the newly built Benchmark data (such as step-wise reasoning paths, fine-grained annotations, preference data) is used to fine-tune or optimize existing models. Including this section significantly adds depth to the paper, because it answers "this Benchmark can not only evaluate, it can also help improve model capability".

### Section 4: Experiments & Empirical Findings (experiments and insights, about 4-5 pages)

The experiments section is the other major part of a Benchmark paper. Unlike a Technique Paper, the purpose of a Benchmark paper's experiments is not to "prove my method is the best" but to **"reveal the capability boundaries of existing models and provide deep insights"**.

**4.1 Experimental Setup.** The following elements need to be stated clearly:

| Element | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Evaluation protocols (Protocols) | How do models receive input? How is the output extracted and evaluated? |
| Evaluation metrics (Metrics) | Which metrics, and why? (e.g., Accuracy, F1@K, MAE, Correlation) |
| Baseline models | Cover representative models: open-source vs. closed-source, different sizes, different families. |
| Prompting strategies | 0-shot, few-shot, CoT, domain knowledge prompting, etc. |
| Human baseline experiment | How is it designed? Who are the participants? What is the experimental protocol? |

**4.2 Overall Performance.** Present one large table (usually the largest table in the paper) covering every model's performance on every metric. Use color or bold to mark the best/second-best results in the table.

**4.3 Fine-grained Analysis (organized by Research Questions).** This is the essence of the experiments section of a Benchmark paper. We suggest using Research Questions or analysis dimensions as subheadings.

| Analysis Dimension | Analysis Content | Recommended Visualization |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Multi-dimensional capability profiling | Differences in model performance across sub-tasks/difficulty levels | Radar chart, grouped bar chart |
| Error type analysis (Error Taxonomy) | Define an error taxonomy and analyze the distribution of the types of mistakes models make | Stacked bar chart, pie chart |
| Model behavior bias | Whether models show systematic bias (e.g., inflated scores, over-conservatism) | Distribution density curves |
| Strategy impact analysis | Effects of different prompting strategies/fine-tuning methods | Comparison table |
| Human vs. LLM comparison | Differences in strengths/weaknesses between human experts and models | Radar chart, comparison bar chart |

**4.4 Case Studies.** Pick 2-4 typical examples and show qualitatively where models succeed and fail in specific scenarios. Case Studies let reviewers intuitively understand the value of the Benchmark and the concrete shortcomings of the models.

**Important writing technique: Finding summaries.** After each important analysis, distill a summary sentence and show it in bold as **Finding X: ...**. This is the signature style of Benchmark papers and helps reviewers quickly grasp the core conclusions. For example:

> **Finding 1.** Few-shot learning and the inclusion of domain knowledge are helpful for LLMs in this task, whereas CoT is more likely to result in slight performance degradation in smaller models.

### Section 5: Discussion & Research Opportunities (discussion and outlook, about 1 page)

Based on the Findings above, discuss future research directions in the field. This section reflects the "forward-looking" value of a Benchmark paper. Typical discussion directions include: how to strengthen models' core capabilities on this task, Human-AI collaboration modes, and future extensions of the Benchmark.

### Section 6: Related Work (about 1 page)

Review related work systematically. **Be sure to provide a comparison table** that clearly shows the differences and advantages of this paper's Benchmark relative to existing Benchmarks along each dimension. The position of Related Work is flexible: it can go in Section 2 (as in VisJudge-Bench) or in the second-to-last section (as in StatQA and nvBench 2.0).

### Section 7: Conclusion (about 0.5 page)

Briefly summarize the core contributions and key findings of the paper.

## 6. Benchmark Paper Writing Checklist

Before submission, check the following points one by one.

### Introduction Checklist

- [ ] Is there a carefully designed Running Example (Figure 1)?

- [ ] Are the key blind spots of existing Benchmarks clearly identified (no more than 3 points)?

- [ ] Is a Benchmark comparison table (Table 1) provided?

- [ ] Are 2-3 Research Questions explicitly listed?

- [ ] Are the Design Considerations explained?

- [ ] Do the contributions correspond to the RQs and the section structure?

### Benchmark Section Checklist

- [ ] Are there clear Design Goals?

- [ ] Is there a complete Pipeline diagram (Figure 2)?

- [ ] Does every step of the Pipeline have a clear description of its input, output, and operations?

- [ ] Is the quality-control strategy described in detail?

- [ ] Are rich dataset statistics shown (figures and tables)?

- [ ] Are data examples provided (Figure)?

### Experiments Section Checklist

- [ ] Do the Baseline models cover representative open-source/closed-source models of different sizes?

- [ ] Is there a large Overall Performance table?

- [ ] Is there a multi-dimensional Fine-grained Analysis?

- [ ] Is there an Error Taxonomy or behavior-bias analysis?

- [ ] Is there a Human vs. LLM comparison experiment? (Depending on the type of task, human performance may not be needed.)

- [ ] Are there Case Studies?

- [ ] Are **Finding X** summaries distilled after the key analyses?

- [ ] Are Research Opportunities discussed?

### Overall Checklist

- [ ] Does the whole paper have a clear logical main line? (Gap → Benchmark → Evaluation → Insights → Opportunities)

- [ ] Are the code and data open-sourced, with links provided in the paper (in the Abstract or in the Contributions summary paragraph of the Intro)?

- [ ] Is there a discussion of Limitations and Future Work?

- [ ] Does the Appendix contain sufficient supplementary material (more examples, complete experimental results, annotation guidelines, etc.)?
