## The Thinking Model for Writing a Paper Introduction (the Introduction Is a Condensed Version of the Whole Paper)

Generally speaking, the Introduction can be regarded as the "compressed version" of the whole paper: use the least space to make the research subject clear, explain thoroughly why the problem is hard, and make clear why our method is necessary. A clear writing organization is as follows. First, use a typical application scenario / running example to introduce the research background and needs. Next, summarize representative existing work and distill the main limitations it exposes under key assumptions, data characteristics, workloads, and system constraints (usually no more than 3 points). On that basis, further characterize the intrinsic properties and hard constraints of the problem (for example scale, dynamism, heterogeneity, end-to-end overhead, correctness/consistency requirements), so as to naturally derive the research goal or problem definition this paper addresses (Our Goal / Problem Formulation), or the core insight (Key Idea) that supports the solution design. Then, state clearly the key challenges in achieving this goal (usually no more than 3 points) and explain why directly applying or simply extending existing methods does not work. Finally, give a method overview (overall framework and key modules) that corresponds one-to-one with the challenges, and close with the contributions: the problem definition/setting (if any), the system/framework design, 1–2 key technical points, and thorough experimental evaluation and analysis.

---

## Flowchart for Writing / Conceiving the Introduction

The core logical chain of the Flowchart:

> **Research background** → **Research frontier (existing methods)** → **Limitations (no more than 3)** → **Key Idea / Our Goal** → **Challenges (no more than 3)** → **Method overview** → **Contributions**

### Quick Positioning: What Type of Paper Is This?

**Technique paper (a new method that solves an existing problem)**
- Main axis: Key Idea / Mechanism
- Goal: one sentence is enough

AFlow is a Technique paper: it proposes a new automated method for an existing problem, "Agentic Workflow design".

| Logical Stage | Original Content and Analysis of the Writing Approach |
|---|---|
| **Research Background** | |
| What is the research scenario? Why does it matter? A clear scenario definition + research motivation are needed | "Large Language Models (LLMs) have emerged as powerful tools for solving complex tasks across various domains, including code generation, data analysis, decision-making, and question answering... However, the rapid advancement of LLMs heavily relies on manually designed agentic workflows – structured sequences of LLM invocations accompanied by detailed instructions. Designing and refining these workflows requires significant human effort, which limits the scalability and adaptability of LLMs to new, complex domains and hinders their ability to transfer skills across diverse tasks." **Writing approach**: Set out the big background: LLMs are impressive, but their capabilities depend heavily on manually designed agentic workflows, and this manual design process is time-consuming and laborious, limiting scalability. |
| **Research Problem Analysis and Interpretation of Its Properties** | |
| Limitation 1 | Recent efforts have focused on automating the discovery of effective agentic workflows to reduce the reliance on human intervention (Khattab et al., 2024; Yüksekg önül et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2023; Hu et al., 2024). Despite these advancements, full automation has not been achieved. For instance, Khattab et al. (2024) require manual workflow setup before automated prompt optimization. Similarly, methods proposed by Yüksekg önül et al. (2024) and Zhuge et al. (2024) fail to capture the full diversity of workflows necessary for a wide range of tasks (Yu et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2024b; Sun et al., 2023), as their optimization objectives struggle to represent the breadth of possible workflows. **Writing approach**: Although some work has begun exploring automated workflows, full automation has not been achieved: existing methods still need a lot of human intervention, or cannot cover a sufficiently diverse range of workflow structures. |
| Limitation 2 | The inability to effectively model diverse workflow structures within these automated systems limits their utility and impact. ADAS (Hu et al., 2024) represents workflows using code, achieving a relatively complete representation. However, due to the efficiency limitations of its linear heuristic search algorithm, ADAS struggles to generate effective workflows within a limited number of iterations. **Writing approach**: Even a method that represents workflows as code (ADAS) is limited in search efficiency by its linear heuristic search and cannot generate effective workflows within a limited number of iterations. |
| Limitation 3 | This paper does not discuss a third limitation. It mainly targets two limitations, "low level of automated workflow orchestration" and "weak ability to model diverse workflows in a unified way", as the motivation for AFlow's method. |
| **Discussion of the Paper's Novelty and Innovative Idea** | |
| Key Idea | AFlow's core insight is to turn the design of agentic workflows into a **code-level search-and-optimization problem**: use MCTS (Monte Carlo Tree Search) to automatically search the code space for the optimal workflow structure, thereby eliminating the need for manual design entirely. |
| Challenges | How do you define a search space (Search Space) that is expressive enough to cover the various possible workflow structures? How do you efficiently find the optimal solution in an enormous search space? |
| Method Overview | **To address these challenges**, we propose AFlow, a Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS)-based framework designed to systematically explore and discover optimal agentic workflows. **AFlow represents workflows as flexible nodes connected by code-based edges, which encapsulate possible relationships such as logical flows, conditions, and dependencies.** These edges allow the workflow to be modeled as a graph or network, offering a powerful structure for capturing complex interactions between LLM invocations. **To enhance the search process and improve efficiency, AFlow introduces a novel concept of operators — predefined, reusable combinations of nodes representing common agentic operations (e.g., Ensemble, Review & Revise).** These operators serve as foundational building blocks for constructing workflows and are integrated into the search space, ensuring that the exploration process leverages known patterns of effective agentic operations. **AFlow** employs the MCTS algorithm to navigate this infinite search space. The framework's workflow optimization process incorporates several key innovations: a soft mixed-probability selection mechanism for node exploration, LLM-driven node expansion to introduce new possibilities, execution evaluation to assess workflow performance, and backpropagation of experience to refine future search iterations. **Writing approach**: The third technical point (MCTS) kills two birds with one stone, solving both "searching slowly" and "searching poorly", and is AFlow's core innovation. |
| Contributions | **We make the following key contributions:** **(1) Problem Formulation:** We formalize the workflow optimization problem, generalizing prior approaches as specific cases. This provides a unified framework for future research at both the node and workflow optimization levels. **(2) AFLOW:** We introduce AFLOW, an MCTS-based method that automatically discovers effective workflows across multiple domains with minimal human intervention. **(3) Extensive Evaluation:** We evaluate AFLOW on six benchmark datasets: HumanEval, MBPP, MATH, GSM8K, HotPotQA, and DROP. AFLOW outperforms manually designed methods by 5.7% and surpasses existing automated approaches by 19.5%. Notably, workflows generated by AFlow enable smaller LLMs to outperform larger models, offering better cost-performance efficiency, with significant implications for real-world applications. |
